### New features

- BibTeX entries copied from a user guide are now keyed by something a reader recognizes, rather than by author-year-title alone. The entry describing the site's own work — its `self` entry, or its `preferred` one when the site publishes no DOI of its own — is keyed by the `lsst.io` subdomain the site is published at (`https://dp2.lsst.io` composes `@dataset{dp2,`); every other entry that has a DOI is keyed by the DOI verbatim (`@misc{10.71929/rubin/3382528,`), which is how `lsst.bib` keys its DataCite records; and an entry with neither falls back to author, year, and title. A new `bibtex_key` field on `[[project.citations]]` pins a key outright (`bibtex_key = "RTN-115"`), for a site whose work an existing bibliography already cites. Keys must be unique across the array a reader pastes into one `.bib` file, and two entries resolving to the same key fail the build with a message naming both. The resolved key also reaches `html_context`, so every surface names the same key.

- The author-year-title BibTeX key now skips a leading `a`, `an`, or `the` when it takes its title word, so *The Vera C. Rubin Observatory Data Butler* is keyed `jenness2022vera` rather than `jenness2022the` — the article was the one word such a key shared with every other title that opens with one.

- A citation whose `date` states a month now writes it into BibTeX: `date = "2026-07"` composes `year = {2026},` then `month = {July},`, as the English month name BibTeX, biblatex, and `lsst.bib` all read. A date stated only to the year writes no `month`, and a date stated to the day writes no `day` field, because BibTeX has none.
